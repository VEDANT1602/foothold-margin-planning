# Primary sources and numerical checks

1. Geiger, Lenz and Urtasun, [2012 paper](https://www.cvlibs.net/publications/Geiger2012CVPR.pdf), §2.3, pp. 2–3. The reference uses the central laser sweep plus five before and after, registration, camera projection and manual exclusions. Stereo-assisted calibration and selection limit independence. The maps retain gaps. The [official Stereo 2012 split](https://www.cvlibs.net/datasets/kitti/eval_stereo_flow.php?benchmark=stereo) has 194 training and 195 benchmark test pairs. This experiment analyzes the public labeled training split exclusively; no leaderboard submission occurred.
2. OpenCV, [4.12 StereoSGBM class](https://docs.opencv.org/4.12.0/d2/d85/classcv_1_1StereoSGBM.html), `create` parameters, three-way mode and disparity output scaling. The code uses OpenCV's implementation, not a reproduction of Hirschmüller's original cost function.
3. Villarreal et al., [author PDF](https://iit-dlslab.github.io/papers/villarreal19ral.pdf), [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1109/LRA.2019.2899434). “Uncertainty Margin” explicitly gives 3 cm nominal foothold prediction uncertainty and 2.5 cm short-term map drift observed after approximately 2 m. “Distance to nominal foothold” chooses the nearest surviving candidate. This experiment adapts that local choice; it omits their collision, whole-body, CNN and online controller. Their measured drift is not a KITTI calibration.
4. Chestnutt et al., [full author copy](https://publications.ri.cmu.edu/storage/publications/pub_files/pub4/chestnutt_joel_2005_1/chestnutt_joel_2005_1.pdf), [institutional record](https://publications.ri.cmu.edu/footstep-planning-for-the-honda-asimo-humanoid), §§I, III. They use finite, state-dependent action sets and limited-horizon A* for ASIMO. The adapted lattice here is fixed heading and monotone, making an exact acyclic shortest path possible. ASIMO's actions, dynamics, collision tests and robot software differ.
5. Dataset licensing and copies. [KITTI's copyright and citation guidance](https://www.cvlibs.net/datasets/kitti/index.php) links CC BY-NC-SA 3.0. No input pixels, laser labels, maps, frames or figures appear in the distributed supplement; generated result tables retain the upstream attribution and share-alike notice. Input links and hashes are in README.

### Context for the historical synthetic counts

The older benchmark's primary bottleneck rows had at most 42 mm half-width with 23 mm foot half-width. The largest possible midpoint clearance is 19 mm. A 25 mm guaranteed-drift allowance therefore structurally rules out every bottleneck route. It cannot establish that such an allowance generally empties real terrain. The corresponding centered-chance Table 3 reports 1,400 layouts, 1,399 admitting at least one feasible graph route and 229 feasible routes rejected. The acceptance count of 1,170 plus those 229 equals 1,399 in that particular table; one additional layout had no feasible graph route. Three selected routes failed despite network feasibility on their layouts. The associated public source is [pinned here](https://github.com/VEDANT1602/foothold-margin-planning/tree/6e6751dac579e40a6da626b90d13b8f34ccf6a48).
